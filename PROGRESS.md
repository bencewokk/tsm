- Stage B: `src/sim/cityprofile.ts` `CityProfile { terrain, origin, history layers, planningStrength, wealth, dominant, site, tradition, story }` drawn from the seed (seed 4821 is the original port city preset). Odd combinations are kept; coherence comes from layers:
  - terrain.ts: coast (sea + bay + river) / river (wide river, wharves) / hills (relief, steep woodland) / plain; every terrain has a river so a port can always have wharves. Origin sites: port (shore/wharf), market-town (radial town by the river), rail-junction (grid town beside a railway + yards), mining (miners' terraces on a hillside, pithead uphill, a mound is raised on flat land), planned-capital (open level site). Older villages follow the origin's character; history layers add a planned quarter (axis boulevard, rond-points, regular grid, civic hall + squares) and garden suburbs; the industrial layer is the redevelopment site (docks / rail yards / mine yard) flagged Flag.Docks.
  - town.ts: street skeletons per quarter style (organic, planned, terraces along contours, radial with ring street, rail grid); planningStrength shifts every profile's roadRegularity. profileAt picks the nearest quarter's core / mid / outer profile, downtown near stations / high value, site profile on the old industrial strip.
  - tradition (south terracotta / north slate / brick / whitewash / sand with flat roofs) swaps the old-fabric palettes; wealth scales heights and greenery; dominant types weight what gets built.
  - render: rail ribbons with sleepers, rond-point islands, rail yards (sheds, sidings with wagons, water tower), mine (headframe, engine house, ore bunkers, spoil heaps), civic hall with dome, grand boulevard fronts (mansards, plinth, cornice), river wharves.
  - events: the docks project is a site project (`requires.site`), its words come from `SITE_TEXT` (docks / rail yards / pithead); capital_quarter event for planned capitals. Start card shows the story and chips.

## Round 3 plan: macro structure and density discipline (in progress)
STATUS (full state in HANDOVER.md): step 1 (density-and-height field, b19afe0) is on master. Step 1b (large plot assembly) is WIP on branch `wip/plot-assembly` (d802959): compiles and runs headless, not verified in the browser. Steps 1c and 2-5 (landscape, road hierarchy, territories, empty structure, satellites) and the visual pass (architect's-model 3D view + map/diagram plan mode) are not started.
Critique: the build reads as a downtown asset pack dropped on a road graph with unrelated settlements. Fix = structure and discipline, NOT more variation (no new building styles, props or landmarks this round). Each numbered step is its own commit; every step is measured (`npm run metrics`, numbers in the table at the end of this section, before vs after).

1. Density-and-height field (`src/sim/field.ts`) computed before any building is placed: one main centre, a few smaller centres, transit corridors. Sequence CBD -> dense mixed-use inner city -> medium-density transit districts -> suburbs and industry -> fringe. The field caps level and height; towers only where land value, job concentration and station accessibility OVERLAP, so the starting city is low and mid-rise around the old core and tall clusters come from the player's transit. The CBD stays compact (radius limited, grows slowly with population). Plots re-subdivide: when the field class of a street changes, vacant and old low plots are re-cut to the new class (split / merge), so mid-ring back-row lawns fill with medium density.
1b. LARGE PLOT ASSEMBLY (required rule, built right after step 1 on the same split / merge machinery): every large development (tower, stadium, arena, factory, campus, big landmark, the docks project) consumes several existing plots. Nothing large appears on a single original plot or on empty fabric.
   - assembly: contiguous plot set scored on land value, field allowance, access and occupant cost; acquired over time, holdouts slow or reshape the site; footprint = union of acquired plots.
   - staging, visible: hoardings -> demolition building by building with rubble -> cleared site with cabins and laydown -> foundations -> structure with cranes (count scales with size) -> facade -> opening. Duration scales with size; only a few staged state changes per tick, chunk rebuilds only when a plot changes.
   - surroundings: local streets through the site are closed or rerouted (road graph + walk catchments updated); forecourt / plaza / arcade appears; venues get a drop-off access road; factories get a yard and freight access.
   - data: assemblies are tracked in the sim (site, plots, phase, progress) and sent through the protocol; landmark / event proposals use the same pipeline and the event dialog lists the plots and buildings a site will clear.
1c. LANDSCAPE that matters (user: "make hills, mountains etc. Make the landscape interesting. But make it impact the game."), done BEFORE road pruning so arterials can follow valleys and passes. Driven by the CityProfile terrain, every seed different.
   - landforms: ridged noise + domain warping + a light erosion / river-carving pass; ridges and mountain ranges at the edge or crossing the map with passes, valleys with the river, steep and gentle sides, plateaus / mesas, isolated hills (fort hill, lookout), coastal cliffs vs beaches, headlands and bays, floodplain with oxbows / wetland, ravines.
   - rendering: hillshade, slope-based materials (rock on steep slopes, scrub, grass, bare rock / snow on peaks), forests on slopes nobody builds on, terraces / vineyards on sunny slopes near old towns, retaining walls where plots sit on slopes, contour lines on the heatmap / plan overlay.
   - gameplay (in the sim): metro cost by terrain and depth (cut-and-cover on flat land cheap; bored tunnel under hills expensive; viaduct across valleys medium and visible; immersed tube / bridge under the river) shown per segment in plan mode; gradient limits flag segments that are too steep; deep stations add escalator time; walk catchments use slope-aware speed (Tobler); roads follow valleys / contours / passes and switch back on slopes; car times include grade; slope limits plot size and density, steep land stays green or terraced villas, views raise land value but not density, floodplain is unbuildable or carries flood risk (park land); ridges split the city into basins so a tunnel line opens a whole basin; passes and river crossings are where satellites form.
   - optional: funicular / cable-car line type (only if it fits the line model, else listed as next); data-driven events (river flood, landslide closing a hillside road, tunnel milestone) filtered by profile.
   - verify: one dramatic oblique per terrain type, plan mode with segment types and costs, hilltop vs flat walk catchment, a city split by a ridge before / after a tunnel line; numbers: cost per km per segment type, catchment area hilltop vs flat, ridership change when a tunnel opens a basin.
2. Prune the road graph and enforce hierarchy: a few continuous arterial spines radiating from the centre (valleys, bridges), collectors per neighbourhood, locals only fill blocks. Remove or downgrade diagonals, short dead ends and triangular leftovers (kept ones become small greens / squares). Render the hierarchy (width, tone, verges, street trees by class); the trip model uses class speed and capacity.
   Hierarchy depends on age and place (user: the hierarchy must read clearly, but in the city centre it makes sense that it is all over the place):
   - OLD CORE and old village satellites stay organic and irregular (narrow winding lanes at odd angles, small squares at junctions, tight blocks): no pruning or straightening there, only true junk (zero-length fragments, overlapping edges, slivers too small for a building). The 1-2 historic main streets (market street, road to the bridge or gate) are a bit wider than the lanes.
   - Arterial spines stop at or wrap around the old core (ring road on the old walls, boulevard along its edge, or a deliberate later cut-through if the history layers suggest it); they do not impose regularity on it.
   - OUTSIDE the core (inner city, transit districts, suburbs, industry) the strict hierarchy and pruning apply: continuous spines, clearly secondary collectors, locals that only fill blocks, few diagonals, no triangle leftovers. Planned quarters are regular by design with clear avenues.
   - Rendering makes classes very distinct from above: arterials wide with a different tone and median / tree lines, collectors medium, locals narrow and light, old-town lanes narrow with a cobble tone rather than asphalt; the contrast between the irregular core and the ordered outer city is itself readable.
   - Measured per zone: diagonals, dead ends and triangles in the core versus outside.
3. Land-use territories with real borders: port / logistics, heavy industry, old core, inner mixed-use, transit suburb, detached suburb, parks and waterfront. Port cities: working port, freight corridors, warehouse belts behind the quays, rail yards, workers' districts. The docks project becomes a bounded redevelopment zone; no housing or towers on working quays.
4. Deliberate empty structure: large parks, rail corridor, floodplain, institutional campus site, industrial yards, green wedges, rendered as edged / planted / fenced / paved ground, never just undeveloped.
5. Satellites with a cause: historical village (organic around church / green), rail town (grid around station), factory town (terraces by the works), planned / commuter suburb (curvilinear). Each has an arterial and/or rail link to the main city and a growth rule, tied to the CityProfile history layers.

Measurements (before = commit 439226b): height and density by distance from the centre and by station access; roads by class, diagonals, dead-end fragments, small triangular faces. Captures: top-down overview (hierarchy reads at a glance), oblique skyline, waterfront, a satellite with its link, a former empty mid-ring area, before / after of seed 4821 plus two other origins, assembly capture sequences (tower and stadium or factory), a street closure with the walk catchment before / after.
