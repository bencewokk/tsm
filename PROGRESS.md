## Step 4: road hierarchy and pruning (done)
Code: `src/sim/hierarchy.ts` (new), strictness and spacing rules in `town.ts` (explore / densify), arterial route cost in `roads.ts`, class capacity in `sim.ts`, class rendering in `render/city.ts`, new road stats in `sim/metrics.ts`. `HIER.on = false` (or `?nohier=1` in the dev URL, `--nohier` in `npm run metrics`) switches back to the old unstructured growth for before / after.
- OLD CORE stays organic: within `coreR` of an original village / origin quarter nothing is straightened or pruned. Arterial edges inside a core become collectors (the historic main streets, 9.5 m cobbled; the other core lanes 6 m cobbled). A ring road (arterial) is laid on the old walls of the origin quarter where the land allows a full circle (not on the harbour town 4821, whose docks and water interrupt it).
- OUTSIDE the core: every new street lies on the district grid (regularity forced to 0.88, no 40-degree tolerance), a branch off an arterial must be a collector and at least 5 cells from the next junction on that arterial, locals only branch off collectors / locals, and a local that meets an arterial is promoted to a collector (so every arterial junction is arterial / collector). Every 6 months (and once at the start) a prune removes streets with no building, landmark or assembly on them that are short stubs (< 3.5 cells), diagonals (> 0.45 rad off the grid) or edges of triangle faces; the plots on them go too; a street is never removed if it would disconnect the network.
- Arterial routing follows valleys and passes (cost for height above the local low point), expensive straight climbs (the path runs along the contours and zigzags up: switchbacks emerge as contour zigzags, not as drawn hairpins) and a bigger penalty for steep ground.
- Class matters in the sim: the road raster holds the class (3 arterial / 2 collector / 1 local) and road capacity per zone weights a cell 2.4 / 1.5 / 1.0, which feeds congestion and car speed.
- Rendering: arterials 20 m, darker, planted median with trees (open stretches only); collectors 11.5 m; locals 7 m and lighter; old-town lanes cobbled.

## Step 6: deliberate empty structure and squares (done)
Code: `src/sim/reserves.ts`, `src/sim/squares.ts` (new), `startSquare` / `finishSquare` in `assembly.ts`, lot field `deco` through the protocol, plot designs in `render/buildings.ts`, rail verges in `render/city.ts`. Dev switches: `RES.on`, `SQ.on`, `?nores=1`, `npm run structure -- 96 [--off]` (a test line from month 24 is built in both modes).
- Reserves (fixed at layout from the city profile, richer / planned cities reserve more): 2-5 large parks in the ring outside the old core (formal: crossing paths, a round bed, rows of trees, kerbed edge), a green wedge (a planted belt with a winding path and a pond running 11-30 cells out on the side away from the port and industry), one campus lawn (paved quad, benches, flagpole). Vacant plots inside a reserve become the park at once, plots cut later become it as they appear; streets still cross them. Industrial plots in the industrial territory are fenced, paved yards (container stacks, gate, pipe rack) 18% of the time. The rail corridor now has grass verges and fence lines on both sides. Vacant building plots are no longer plain lawns: each is kept as a kerbed lawn with trees, allotment beds with a shed, or a small car park.
- SQUARES: `squarePressure = 0.22 ped + 0.24 retail + 0.14 landmark + 0.20 transit + 0.10 water/crossing - 0.34 existing public space`, every term normalised to 0..1 (pedestrianCentrality = junction degree x road importance, retail = commercial jobs within 3 cells / 300, landmark = open landmarks within 6 cells, transit = riders of stations within 4 cells / 2000, water = junction at the shore or an arterial near a river, existing = parks and squares within 4 cells). Evaluated once a year (month 18 on), at non-core, non-industrial junctions; a square forms only at pressure >= 0.36 for two evaluations in a row (hysteresis 0.08), at least 9 cells from any other square, at most one a year, and it never goes away. The site is carved at the junction by buying 2-6 adjacent cheap plots (size from pressure) through the assembly pipeline (hoardings, demolition, paving), then paved with bands, a fountain, benches, lamps and trees. Feedback: shops are favoured next to a square (+0.3 on the commercial score within 2 cells), it counts as public space for the next evaluation and as an amenity in land value (the park raster).
